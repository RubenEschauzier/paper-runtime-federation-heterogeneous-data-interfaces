\subsection{Delegating Conjunctive Queries}
Under this query execution model, we now show that correct delegation to composite
sources can be established under \emph{incomplete} discovery.

\begin{lemma}[Claimed triples occur only in their claimant]
\label{lem:exclusive}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:disjoint}, let $c \in \Dd$ claim $\tau$. Then $\tau$ is
admitted from no source $d \neq c$. Consequently, for every $A \subseteq \Dd$,
$\tau \in G^{\alpha}_A$ implies $c \in A$ and $\tau \in \dataf(c)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $\tau$ is admitted from some $d \neq c$. The second clause of
\eqref{eq:admitted} fails, as $c \in \Dd \setminus \{d\}$ claims $\tau$. So the first
holds and $d$ claims $\tau$, contradicting Assumption~\ref{as:disjoint}.
\end{proof}

A delegated composite answers over all of $\dataf(c)$, so its answer must be filtered
to what the claim semantics admits. The engine can always check the claim of $c$, as
$\alpha_c$ is known once $c$ is retrieved. For a BGP $B'$ we therefore keep the solutions
whose matched triples $c$ all claims,
\[
  \Phi_c(\Omega) \;=\; \bigl\{\, \mu \in \Omega \;\bigm|\; \alpha_c(\tau) = 1
  \text{ for every } \tau \in \mu(B') \,\bigr\}.
\]

Theorem~\ref{th:undetermined} shows that the condition of
Proposition~\ref{prop:correct-delegation} cannot be checked under incomplete discovery, as a
source behind the frontier may hold triples that match $B'$. For triples that a
composite $c$ claims, this cannot happen. By Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusive}, such a triple is
admitted only from $c$, so no other source contributes it to $G = G^{\alpha}_{\Dd}$,
whether it has been discovered or not. Filtering the answer of $c$ with $\Phi_c$ thus
yields exactly the solutions of $B'$ that consist of triples $c$ claims. We take the
remaining solutions from the rest of the plan, which evaluates $B'$ as usual and drops
what $c$ already supplies. With
\[
  \overline{\Phi}_c(\Omega) \;=\; \Omega \setminus \Phi_c(\Omega)
\]
the solutions $\mu$ with at least one triple of $\mu(B')$ that $c$ does not claim, let
\[
  R^{*} \;:=\; \Phi_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{\dataf(c)}\bigr) \;\cup\;
  \overline{\Phi}_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{G}\bigr).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Delegation to a claim]
\label{th:claim-delegation}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:disjoint}, let $c \in \Dd(T)$ be a composite and $B' \subseteq B$
non-empty. Then
\[
  \Phi_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{\dataf(c)}\bigr) \;=\; \Phi_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{G}\bigr),
\]
and this equality is determined at $T$. Consequently, $R^{*} = \ev{B'}{G}$ with disjoint
terms, so delegating $B'$ to $c$ is correct for every $B'$ and produces no solution twice.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Write $H_c := \{\, \tau \in \Tt \mid \alpha_c(\tau) = 1 \,\}$ for the triples $c$ claims.
  For any graph $G'$, a solution $\mu \in \ev{B'}{G'}$ is kept by $\Phi_c$ exactly when
  $\mu(B') \subseteq G' \cap H_c$, so by \eqref{eq:bgp-match}
  \[
    \Phi_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{G'}\bigr) \;=\; \ev{B'}{G' \cap H_c}.
  \]
  Applying this to both sides, it suffices to show $\dataf(c) \cap H_c = G \cap H_c$.
  ($\subseteq$) A triple of $\dataf(c)$ that $c$ claims is admitted from $c$ by the first
  clause of \eqref{eq:admitted}, and $c \in \Dd$, so it lies in $G$. ($\supseteq$) A triple
  of $G$ that $c$ claims lies in $\dataf(c)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusive}.

  The left-hand side depends only on $\dataf(c)$ and $\alpha_c$, both observed once $c$ is
  retrieved. As the argument holds in every state satisfying
  Assumption~\ref{as:disjoint}, the claimed fragment of the reference answer is known
  at $T$.

  For the consequence, substituting the equality into $R^{*}$ gives
  \[
    R^{*} \;=\; \Phi_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{G}\bigr) \cup \overline{\Phi}_c\bigl(\ev{B'}{G}\bigr)
    \;=\; \ev{B'}{G},
  \]
  as $\Phi_c$ and $\overline{\Phi}_c$ split $\ev{B'}{G}$ into two disjoint parts, which are
  the two terms of $R^{*}$. A BGP evaluates to the join of its patterns, and $\bowtie$ is
  associative and commutative, so $B' \subseteq B$ gives
  \[
    \ev{B}{G} \;=\; \ev{B'}{G} \bowtie \ev{B \setminus B'}{G}
    \;=\; R^{*} \bowtie \ev{B \setminus B'}{G}.
  \]
  This is the correctness condition of Definition~\ref{def:delegation}, with $R^{*}$ as
  the answer to $B'$ and the claim semantics as reference
  (Definition~\ref{def:claim-semantics}).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{th:claim-delegation} recovers Proposition~\ref{prop:correct-delegation} under
incomplete discovery. Restricted to the triples $c$ claims, $c$ is the only relevant
source, by construction rather than by observation. The adversary of
Theorem~\ref{th:undetermined} can still place triples behind the frontier, but a triple
that $c$ claims is not admitted from any other source, so it cannot add solutions to the
claimed fragment. These solutions also never need to be retracted. Every triple of
$\mu(B')$ is held by its claimant, so it is covered (Definition~\ref{def:covered}), and
the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:covered-stable} applies.

Correctness thus holds for every $B'$. What delegation saves, and how the base plan
recognises results that $c$ already supplies, depends on the admissibility function.
Section~\ref{sec:shapes} settles this for namespace claims.
